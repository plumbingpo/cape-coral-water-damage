Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. After extraction removes the standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in structural materials, including drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, insulation and other building components. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

The structural drying technicians at Cape Coral Restoration Co are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Daily monitoring is built into how we dry a structure, and it relies on calibrated meters, thermal cameras and thermo-hygrometers. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Those readings guide daily equipment adjustments that keep the drying as efficient as it can be. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

Warning Signs for Structural Drying

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Swollen trim and baseboards separating at the seams
  • Materials breaking down in a spot that got wet before and was not dried by professionals
  • Wallboard with water stains, puffiness or a spongy feel
  • Trapped moisture often announces itself with a persistent musty odor after cleanup
  • Condensation forming on windows in the area that was wet
  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try
  • Materials that feel damp or cool to the touch even after visible water is gone

Why Hire a Professional for Structural Drying?

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. To do it professionally, you have to know how each material takes on and gives up moisture, work out the grain depression, meaning the moisture removal capacity, for the job, and check progress every day. Get it wrong in one direction and mold feeds on leftover moisture, get it wrong in the other and wood or similar materials are damaged by over-drying.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. Between the investment in equipment and the training needed to run it, it is no surprise that DIY drying falls well short of a professional job.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Dehumidifiers lose efficiency when outside air gets in, so keep drying areas closed up
  • New odors or wet areas during the drying period should be reported to your technician right away
  • Trust the moisture meter readings, because surfaces can feel dry while moisture remains deep in materials
  • Let air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, 24/7, until the technician removes them
  • Keep belongings off the machines and out of the path of the air movers
  • Leave the drying equipment on even if the noise bothers you, since each hour counts

Mapping the Moisture

2

Equipment Deployment

Commercial LGR dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are strategically positioned based on our moisture map. The materials being dried determine how we set the airflow and the temperature.
